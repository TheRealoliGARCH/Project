\begin{abstract}
A discount factor converts a future unit of currency into its time-zero present value. Under continuous compounding, the discount factor at maturity $m$ is $D(m)=\exp[-m y(m)]$, where $y(m)$ is the continuously compounded zero-coupon spot yield. This paper develops a reproducible framework for comparing Greek and Italian sovereign discount curves using the Nelson--Siegel--Svensson (NSS) model. The central methodological distinction is between coupon-bond yields-to-maturity, which summarize multiple cash flows, and zero-coupon spot rates, which directly determine discount factors. The repository workflow executes the empirical pipeline, validates the codebase, generates a results section directly from reproducible outputs, and compiles the resulting paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A financial claim payable at a future date is not generally equivalent in present value to the same nominal claim payable today. For maturity $m$ and continuously compounded spot yield $y(m)$,
\begin{equation}
D(m)=e^{-m y(m)}.
\end{equation}
The central empirical question is how to construct comparable model-implied discount curves from documented sovereign-bond observations.

\section{Yield-to-Maturity and Zero-Coupon Curves}
For a coupon-bearing government bond $i$,
\begin{equation}
P_i=\sum_{j=1}^{N_i-1}C_{ij}D(t_{ij})+(C_{iN_i}+F_i)D(T_i).
\end{equation}
Its yield-to-maturity compresses several cash-flow discount factors into a scalar internal rate of return. Therefore a benchmark yield-to-maturity cross-section is not, without additional assumptions, itself a directly observed zero-coupon discount curve.

\section{Nelson--Siegel--Svensson Model}
Let $\theta=(\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3,\tau_1,\tau_2)$. Define
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(\mu)=\frac{1-e^{-\mu}}{\mu}.
\end{equation}
The NSS spot-yield curve is
\begin{align}
y(m;\theta)&=\beta_0+\beta_1\Lambda\left(\frac{m}{\tau_1}\right)
+\beta_2\left[\Lambda\left(\frac{m}{\tau_1}\right)-e^{-m/\tau_1}\right]\\
&\quad+\beta_3\left[\Lambda\left(\frac{m}{\tau_2}\right)-e^{-m/\tau_2}\right],
\end{align}
with model-implied discount factor
\begin{equation}
D(m;\theta)=e^{-m y(m;\theta)}.
\end{equation}

\section{Two-Country Comparison}
For $c\in\{\mathrm{GR},\mathrm{IT}\}$,
\begin{equation}
\hat\theta_c=\arg\min_{\theta_c}\sum_{i=1}^{n_c}\left[\hat y_{i,c}-y(m_{i,c};\theta_c)\right]^2.
\end{equation}
The bilateral comparison is
\begin{equation}
\Delta D(m)=\hat D_{\mathrm{GR}}(m)-\hat D_{\mathrm{IT}}(m).
\end{equation}
Under continuous compounding, for $m>0$,
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{GR}}(m)>D_{\mathrm{IT}}(m)
\iff y_{\mathrm{GR}}(m)<y_{\mathrm{IT}}(m).
\end{equation}
This follows from the strict monotonicity of the exponential function.

\section{Interpretation and Limitations}
The generated curves are NSS model outputs fitted under the repository's declared transformation convention. They must not be described as directly observed zero-coupon discount factors. Differences can reflect sovereign risk, inflation expectations, liquidity, maturity segmentation, monetary conditions, and estimation asymmetry. A market discount curve is also distinct from a social discount function used in welfare economics.

\section{Conclusion}
The repository now treats reproducibility as an executable property: empirical inputs generate estimates, estimates generate the bilateral discount-factor comparison, tests validate the implementation, and successful validation generates the paper artifact. The central object remains
\begin{equation}
D(m)=e^{-m y(m)}.
\end{equation}

\section*{Glossary}
\begin{description}
\item[Discount factor] Present value of one unit payable at a future maturity.
\item[Spot yield] Continuously compounded yield on a zero-coupon claim.
\item[Yield to maturity] Internal rate of return summarizing a bond's contractual cash flows.
\item[NSS model] Nelson--Siegel--Svensson parametric representation of a term structure.
\item[RMSE] Root mean squared fitting error.
\item[Reproducibility] Ability to regenerate outputs from tracked inputs and declared procedures.
\end{description}
